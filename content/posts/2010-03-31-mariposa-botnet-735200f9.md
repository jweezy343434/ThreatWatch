**What Happened**
The Mariposa botnet was a sophisticated malware operation that infected millions of computers worldwide between 2008-2010. The botnet utilized the Butterfly Bot (also called Palevo) malware, which spread through removable drives, instant messaging, and peer-to-peer networks. Once infected, compromised machines became part of a distributed network controlled by cybercriminals who could steal credentials, financial data, and personal information from infected systems.

**Impact**
Mariposa demonstrated the massive scale and economic damage botnets can inflict on critical infrastructure and organizations. The botnet compromised over 13 million IP addresses across 190+ countries, including systems belonging to major corporations, government agencies, and financial institutions. Infected machines experienced degraded performance, data theft, and served as launching points for additional attacks. The botnet's ability to update itself and download additional malware made it particularly persistent and dangerous.

**Actions**
Organizations should implement comprehensive endpoint protection with real-time malware detection and behavioral analysis. Disable autorun features on removable media and restrict USB device usage through group policies. Deploy network monitoring to identify suspicious botnet command-and-control communications. Maintain updated antivirus signatures and conduct regular system scans. Educate users about safe computing practices, particularly regarding removable media and file sharing. Establish incident response procedures for potential botnet infections, including network isolation protocols and forensic capabilities to assess compromise scope.
